# Ontology Drift as a Threat to Validity in Cyber Threat Intelligence Analytics: Measurement, Consequences, and a Reporting Discipline for MITRE ATT&CK

## Abstract

Security machine learning spent a decade learning to control for bias in its data. Time-aware splits, realistic base rates, drift detection and conformal rejection are now expected of any credible evaluation. All of that discipline assumes something it never states: that the label space is fixed. In cyber threat intelligence it is not. MITRE ATT&CK, the vocabulary in which almost every TTP-level claim is written, is a versioned ontology that adds, revokes, deprecates, re-cuts, re-parents and silently rewrites its own terms between releases. We name the resulting failure mode *ontology drift*, formalize it as a change in the label space rather than in the distribution over a fixed label space, and show that it is a first-class threat to the validity of CTI analytics — measurable, conclusion-changing, only partly repairable, and undetected by every mechanism the ecosystem currently deploys.

We measure drift across 19 major Enterprise releases, 19 Mobile and 12 ICS releases (Table 1). Identifier churn is episodic: the v6.0-to-v7.0 boundary has an identifier-set Jaccard of 0.222 and pre-restructuring cohorts have half-lives of 0.4 to 2.2 years, while every post-2020 cohort retains above 0.97 of its identifiers (Tables 2, 3). Semantic churn never stopped: 0.465 of v11.0's identifier-stable techniques have edited descriptions by v19.0, and ATT&CK's own version field detects text change with precision 0.447 and recall 0.641 (Tables 4, 13). Of 3,074 group-technique edges added for pre-existing groups, 0.323 are bookkeeping rather than intelligence (Table 5). Downstream, a controlled attribution experiment shows a drift penalty of 42.2 points for v1.0 vocabularies and 1.6 points across a single modern release boundary, with the named actor changing in 0.722 and 0.022 of observations respectively (Tables 6, 10). A frozen detection capability that claimed 97.0% coverage at v6.0 measures 17.4% at v19.0 (Table 8). Across four deployed CTI corpora, 0.498 of rcATT's label vocabulary is invalid today and no ATT&CK release makes CTIBench-ATE's labels simultaneously valid (Table 9); zero of nineteen scanned documentation files declare an ATT&CK release (Table 12).

We meet the strongest published objection — that products disagree on the ATT&CK label for the same behaviour within one pinned release — by experimental design rather than by argument, and verify separability with a noise-injection factorial that also shows the headline penalties are upper bounds. We show that identifier normalization repairs legacy identifiers and buys almost nothing modern, and that the residual is precisely the part nobody detects. The contribution is a threat, its control, and a four-line reporting contract that makes the vocabulary of a CTI artefact a declared, checkable property of that artefact.

## 1. Introduction

The most likely reader of this paper has published a result that is not comparable to the result it is compared against, and does not know it.

Make it concrete. Suppose you published a TTP-based attribution system in 2019 and reported that it beat the prior state of the art on a labelled corpus. A successor published in 2023 reports a higher number on the same corpus under the same metric. Between those dates the label space in which both systems express their predictions lost roughly half its identifiers in a single release, gained a level of abstraction that did not previously exist, and rewrote three-quarters of the descriptions of the terms that survived. Neither paper declares which ATT&CK release it used; neither could, in practice, because no venue asks. That is not a weak comparison. It is a comparison between two measurement instruments whose readings are reported in the same units by convention alone.

Security machine learning has an unusually mature literature on this class of error. TESSERACT established that malware classifiers evaluated without temporal and spatial constraints report accuracies that do not survive deployment, and gave the field a vocabulary in which such failures can be named and controlled [67]. Arp et al. formalized ten recurring methodological pitfalls and showed that much published work at top venues commits several of them [35]. Concept-drift work extended this into deployed practice: CADE detects and explains drifting samples [22], conformal evaluation supplies calibrated rejection [70], dataset-shift taxonomies distinguish covariate shift from prior-probability shift from real concept drift [33], and malware-specific drift adaptation is now its own subfield [50].

Every one of those instruments operates on a fixed label space. Dataset-shift taxonomy is defined over the joint distribution with the class set held constant [33]; the temporal constraints in TESSERACT constrain sample timestamps, not class meanings [67]; conformal rejection calibrates non-conformity against a known class set [70]. Where the class set itself changes, class-incremental learning addresses only the additive quadrant [24], and only recent work on continual learning with evolving class ontologies treats relabelling as a first-class phenomenon [43]. Within security, one line of work explicitly frames ATT&CK version updates as concept drift and proposes incremental learning as the remedy [42] — closer than anything else in the field, and still treating the ontology as a source of distributional disturbance rather than as the coordinate system the measurement is expressed in.

ATT&CK is that coordinate system. It is the label space of TTP extraction [71, 72], threat-actor attribution [23, 12], LLM CTI benchmarks [30, 29, 13], detection engineering and coverage reporting [74, 46], and the mitigation mappings that justify security spend [58, 64]. It is also, by its own design documentation, non-stationary: inclusion criteria evolve with observed adversary behaviour, and the abstraction level at which a behaviour is described is an acknowledged design decision rather than a fact about the world [14]. A practitioner defence of ATT&CK's structure argues the same point from the other side — that ATT&CK is an ontology rather than a taxonomy, and ontologies are expected to evolve [77]. We agree with both. That is the problem, not the answer.

This paper makes four claims. **First, ontology drift is a distinct, nameable threat to validity that belongs in the same catalogue as temporal bias and sampling bias.** Section 4 formalizes it: concept drift is a change in the conditional distribution over a fixed class set; ontology drift is a change in the class set itself together with a partial, typed correspondence across releases. A system perfectly robust to concept drift can still report an incomparable number, because the incomparability is in the units. **Second, the threat is measurable.** Section 6 quantifies drift across the full published release history of three domains and yields a two-clock model: a slow episodic referential clock and a fast continuous intensional clock. The catastrophic identifier numbers the literature already cites [63] are one event, and a paper that headlines them deserves the rebuttal that the event is six years old; the numbers that survive that rebuttal are the semantic ones. **Third, drift changes conclusions, not merely scores.** The template comes from outside security: Tomczak et al. froze a biological pipeline, swept the Gene Ontology version, and showed the biological conclusions moved [69]. Showing a score moves is a hygiene complaint. Showing the named actor changes, the top-ranked mitigation changes, and a coverage claim falls by four-fifths is a validity finding. **Fourth, the repair is partial, and the unrepairable part is the invisible part.** Identifier arithmetic repairs identifiers; it says nothing about whether two identifiers mean the same thing, and meaning is what moves.

We concede two things loudly, because the argument does not survive without them. MITRE's versioning apparatus is better than most scientific vocabularies: immutable per-release bundles, typed revocation edges with retained tombstones, a published change-computation tool, a version-stamped layer format [1, 7, 15, 34]. For Enterprise techniques the identifier accounting is complete — every revoked identifier in our corpus resolves. And the synchronic labelling noise floor is large: detection products assign different ATT&CK labels to the same behaviour within a pinned release [74], and most ATT&CK groups have no technique unique to them [59], so TTP attribution is weakly identifiable before drift enters. We do not claim drift dominates that noise. We claim it is separable by construction, we verify that with a factorial rather than asserting it, and we report the resulting bounds as bounds.

## 2. Background: ATT&CK as a Versioned Ontology

ATT&CK is published as immutable per-release STIX bundles at stable paths, and its usage contract recommends the static-copy workflow explicitly, on the grounds that a downloaded copy does not change underneath an automated pipeline [1]. Pinning is not a community workaround; it is the documented first-class path. Any argument that opens by accusing MITRE of negligence is refuted by that one document.

The retirement contract is likewise normative. Objects no longer useful to track are *deprecated*; objects replaced by another object are *revoked* and carry a typed `revoked-by` relationship to the replacement; both classes are retained in every bundle so dependent workflows do not break, and a resolver and a retired-object filter ship alongside [1]. MITRE also publishes `diffStix`, whose change taxonomy includes a `patches` class covering objects changed while their version field stays the same, and whose documentation concedes that version discipline broke historically [7, 34, 48]. ATT&CK Navigator, the canonical coverage format, defines a `versions` object carrying the content version a layer was authored against [15] and documents a layer-upgrade flow [16]. CTID funds ATT&CK Sync specifically to flag mappings affected by a release [5] — an institutional concession that staying in sync is an operational burden.

Three structural facts matter for what follows.

**The March 2020 restructuring.** ATT&CK v7.0 introduced sub-techniques, a second level of abstraction beneath the technique. This was a re-cut, not an addition: techniques spanning several distinct behaviours were revoked and replaced by parent-plus-children structures, and existing mappings were re-pointed to the most specific applicable child. Table 2 quantifies it. It is the single most-cited instability in the ATT&CK literature [63] and also the best-handled event in the corpus.

**The v19 tactic re-cut.** In April 2026 ATT&CK retired *Defense Evasion* as a tactic, splitting its content between a renamed *Stealth* and a new *Defense Impairment* [78]; the industry narrative was one of rapid, unproblematic migration. The mechanism matters as much as the magnitude. The tactic identifier TA0005 was recycled in place, keeping its identifier while its name and referent changed, and no revocation edges exist on tactic objects at all. A pipeline joining on TA0005 across that boundary compares two different concepts with nothing to follow.

**The 1:1 revocation relation.** `revoked-by` is total over revoked Enterprise techniques and never one-to-many. That is a stronger property than the literature usually credits, and it is also the source of a specific defect: a split cannot be expressed by a function into a single successor, so MITRE encodes splits as many-to-one merges onto whichever survivor is narrowest. Eight merge targets in the current bundle absorb twenty predecessor identifiers, the largest absorbing five. The crosswalk is mechanically total and semantically lossy, and the loss is unannounced — a design consequence of the relation's arity, not an oversight in its population.

Finally, where ATT&CK lives in practice. The Navigator version field is optional and silently defaults to the current release [15]; six of eight sample layers in MITRE's own Navigator repository carry no version [18]; and the most widely used layer-generation tool omits the field by default, stamping its own build constant instead [79]. The declared version is the field the ecosystem most reliably leaves empty.

## 3. Related Work

**CTI quality.** Feed-level evaluations find low overlap and limited timeliness across commercial and open feeds [41, 45]; quality frameworks enumerate dimensions and propose instruments [60, 76]; automated and community-driven schemes score feeds against weighted criteria [27, 38]; interview work documents quality assurance inside CTI teams [40]; a measurement-based survey reaches the same enumeration [11]. The recurring dimensions are accuracy, completeness, timeliness, relevance, provenance and interoperability. None defines a dimension over the *reference vocabulary*. We searched specifically for one and did not find it; consistent with our evidence discipline that is a bounded absence, not a proof.

**ATT&CK as an object of study.** A systematization established that ATT&CK-based results are frequently non-comparable and flagged temporal instability as an open problem [63]; a subsequent survey set the bar a contribution must clear [47]. Both treat version instability as a known hazard; neither quantifies its effect on a downstream conclusion, which is the gap this paper occupies.

**Coverage claims.** The strongest empirical work shows ATT&CK coverage is not comparable across detection products, because vendors labelling the same behaviour choose different techniques within one pinned release [74]. MITRE's own evaluation programme refuses to emit a single coverage score [46]; independent re-analysis argues per-technique tallies are the wrong unit [62]. Practitioners are blunter: a 100% claim is a red flag [80], heatmaps count rules rather than coverage under several incompatible denominators [84], and vendor-side commentary enumerates the padding techniques [82]. Mitigation coverage has a ceiling set by the control catalogue [58], CTID's Summiting the Pyramid replaces techniques with implementations as the denominator [64], and the ad hoc construction of coverage layers over a versioned data-source mapping is documented from the practitioner side [81]. This literature establishes cross-product incomparability at a fixed version; it does not establish cross-version incomparability at a fixed product, which is our Section 7.4.

**CTI benchmarks.** CTIBench introduced a multi-task benchmark including ATT&CK technique extraction, designed around authoritative sources [30]. Successors grow the question set under a stable identifier [29], move to live-API evaluation explicitly to counter staleness [13], and expand the label space substantially [6]. Sequence-level benchmarks add tactic ordering as a drift surface [17]; large instruction corpora span two decades of reports without declaring a release [61]; multi-label work now declares existing annotation sets non-gold [52] and a fork verifies labels against official descriptions [73]; synthesis work targets the long tail [65]. Answer-key decay in static benchmarks has been quantified outside security [75]. What none of this work does is declare the ATT&CK release its gold labels encode, which Section 9 measures.

**TTP extraction and attribution.** TTPDrill remains the pre-sub-technique baseline later systems compare against [71]; TTPHunter and TTPXHunter expand the class count without declaring a release [72]; rcATT's released label space is a 2019-era flat vocabulary [44, 8]; CAPTAIN represents current TTP-sequence attribution, also undeclared [23]; an APT attribution survey organizes the field by artefact type rather than ontology version [12]; AttacKG works against an ontology frozen as a dated HTML scrape [2]. An audit of six TTP systems finds five different ATT&CK ontologies and two declared versions [3], and one published remapping dataset declares a target release while carrying identifiers revoked five releases earlier [10]. Two results bound what TTP attribution can achieve at all: two-thirds of ATT&CK groups have no group-specific behaviour [59], and LLM agents reproduce documented APT profiles at 55-80% precision [66]. Multi-report evaluation finds many extractor errors are between same-tactic, description-overlapping techniques [20] — a rival explanation for label instability that Section 7.2 must separate from drift, as must vendor fragmentation more generally [68]. Telemetry-grounded evaluation has been proposed as the structurally drift-resistant alternative [28].

**Concept drift and evolving label spaces.** The formal machinery is mature [33] and its security instantiation well developed [22, 67, 70, 50]. Open and unstable class sets have been studied in malware family labelling [51], and label normalization across inconsistent vendor vocabularies has prior art in AVClass [19]. Class-incremental learning covers additive change [24]; continual learning with evolving class ontologies covers relabelling [43]; IncreTTP is the closest security work [42]. The boundary is the point: this literature stops where the label space starts moving in non-additive ways, and synthesising that boundary is what motivates our formalization [21].

**Ontology engineering and other versioned vocabularies.** Everything ATT&CK lacks exists elsewhere. OBO Foundry mandates identifier uniqueness, non-reuse and persistence [53], version IRIs and release immutability [55], and referent stability with exact-versus-inexact successors [54]. OWL supplies `versionIRI`, `priorVersion` and `backwardCompatibleWith` [57]. Change management is recognized as the core task of ontology versioning [56], COnto-Diff types complex change operations as evolution mappings [26], and vocabulary churn versus instance-data usage has been studied as two time series in knowledge graphs [37]. Most directly, Tomczak et al. showed Gene Ontology evolution changes the interpretation of biological experiments [69]. Within security, vocabulary instability is documented for CVE lifecycle states [31], CVE-CWE-CPE abstraction-ladder mappings [32], CVSS score fragmentation [39] and NVD data quality [25].

**The gap.** The field has the diff and lacks the measurement. MITRE publishes change enumeration [34]; CTID publishes affected-mapping flags [5]; the SoK names temporal instability as open [63]. Nobody has measured what a release costs an analytic built on the previous one, and no quality framework names the vocabulary as a quality-bearing property.

## 4. Problem Formalization and Drift Taxonomy

### 4.1 Setup

Let $V$ index the published releases of an ATT&CK domain. A release determines a label space $\mathcal{L}_V$, the set of technique identifiers live in it, together with an *intension* map $\iota_V : \mathcal{L}_V \to \mathcal{D}$ assigning each identifier the description, tactic assignment, platform set and detection guidance that define what it denotes. A CTI analytic is a function $f : \mathcal{X} \to 2^{\mathcal{L}_V}$ from evidence to a set of technique labels. A CTI artefact is a pair $(A, V)$ with $A \subseteq \mathcal{L}_V$. In deployed practice $V$ is almost never recorded (Table 12), so what circulates is $A$ alone.

Two artefacts are *comparable* when expressed in the same label space under the same intension map, and two results are comparable when the analytics producing them are. This is not a strong requirement. It is the ordinary requirement that measurements reported in the same units be reported in the same units.

### 4.2 Why this is not concept drift

Concept drift is defined over a fixed label space: the standard taxonomy decomposes change in $P_t(X, Y)$ into covariate shift, prior-probability shift and real concept drift, all with $\mathcal{Y}$ constant [33]. The security instruments built on it inherit the assumption — temporal splits control when samples were drawn [67], detectors compare feature-space distances to a reference distribution [22], conformal rejection calibrates against a known class set [70] — and the pitfall catalogue contains no entry for a label space that changes between the training and evaluation corpora [35], because at the time no security label space was expected to.

Class-incremental learning relaxes the assumption in one direction only: classes are added and previously learned classes retain their meaning [24]. That covers ATT&CK's additions and nothing else. The only formalism that comes close is continual learning with evolving class ontologies, which models old data being relabelled under a refined class structure [43] — and even that assumes the refinement is known, typed and complete. In ATT&CK it is partially typed, occasionally lossy, and for the component that matters most, entirely unrecorded.

The distinction is this. Concept drift moves the data under a fixed ruler; ontology drift moves the ruler. A model that is perfectly robust to concept drift — retrained continuously, conformally calibrated, deployed under a rigorous time-aware split — still produces an incomparable number if its label space changed and it did not say so. Robustness is not the remedy, because the failure is not in the predictions but in the interpretation of the outputs.

### 4.3 Six drift operators

We type ontology drift by the operators transforming $(\mathcal{L}_V, \iota_V)$ into $(\mathcal{L}_W, \iota_W)$ for $W > V$.

**O1. Addition.** $\ell \in \mathcal{L}_W \setminus \mathcal{L}_V$ with no predecessor. The only operator benign for backward comparison and the only one the class-incremental literature handles [24]. It is not benign forward: a growing coverage denominator makes a frozen capability's percentage fall with no change in the capability, which is the mechanism behind Table 8.

**O2. Deprecation.** $\ell \in \mathcal{L}_V \setminus \mathcal{L}_W$ with no successor; the concept is withdrawn. ATT&CK marks these and retains the object [1]. A pipeline that filters retired objects drops the label; one that does not silently carries a dead term.

**O3. Revocation with a typed successor.** $\ell \in \mathcal{L}_V \setminus \mathcal{L}_W$ with a `revoked-by` edge to some live $\ell'$. This is the well-handled case and the one the ecosystem's defence rests on. In our corpus every revoked Enterprise identifier resolves (Table 3); the relation is a total function into the live label space.

**O4. Re-cut.** A concept is split across successors, or several are merged into one. Because `revoked-by` is a function and never one-to-many, a split is representable only as many-to-one merges onto the narrowest survivor. The relation *can* express the v19 re-cut — it is simply not true that it cannot — and the defect is annotation rather than expressiveness: the merge is unlabelled, so a consumer applying the crosswalk mechanically cannot distinguish an exact successor from a narrowing. Ontology engineering solved this by splitting the successor relation into exact and inexact variants [54] and typing complex change operations as first-class evolution mappings [26]. ATT&CK has one untyped relation where the literature specifies at least two.

**O5. Cross-layer reassignment.** A concept moves between levels: technique to sub-technique, sub-technique to technique, technique to tactic. In the current Enterprise bundle 119 of 149 revocation edges demote a top-level technique onto a sub-technique and seven promote in the opposite direction, with no abstraction-level annotation, so a consumer cannot detect that the granularity of its assertion changed. The v19 promotion of a technique to a tactic is not representable at all, because a revocation edge cannot point a technique at a tactic. Tactic reassignment at the instance level is also unsignalled: 198 identifier-stable techniques changed tactic in a single transition (Table 2), and tactic objects carry no revocation edges.

**O6. Intensional rewriting.** $\ell \in \mathcal{L}_V \cap \mathcal{L}_W$ with $\iota_V(\ell) \neq \iota_W(\ell)$. The identifier survives; what it denotes moves. This operator is invisible to every identifier-based mechanism in the ecosystem — the crosswalk, the retired-object filter, the layer version stamp. It is the operator our protocol cannot repair and the one Section 6.2 shows never stops.

### 4.4 Referential and intensional; signalled and unsignalled

O1-O5 are *referential*: they change which identifiers exist and how they correspond. O6 is *intensional*: it changes what a surviving identifier means. That is the paper's organizing axis, because the two components have different dynamics (Section 6), different downstream consequences (Section 7) and different repair prospects (Section 8).

Cutting across it is a second distinction we consider more practically important. Call an operator *signalled* if the published artefact carries a machine-readable marker a consumer can test for. O2 and O3 are signalled. O4 and O5 are partially signalled: the edges exist but carry no type, so the consumer learns that something changed and not what. O6 is unsignalled. ATT&CK's object version field is the only candidate marker, and as a detector of description change it has precision 0.447 and recall 0.641 (Section 6.3). A detector at that operating point is not a detector: a consumer trusting it misses a third of real rewrites and chases a majority of false alarms.

This is the sharpest statement of the thesis. The component of ontology drift that is well handled is the component everybody points at, and the component that is not handled at all is the component nobody can see.

### 4.5 The two-clock model

Drift does not run at one rate. Let $\sigma_V(t)$ be the fraction of a cohort live at $V$ still live at $t$, and $\tau_V(t)$ the fraction whose intension has substantively changed. The referential clock, characterized by $\sigma$, is episodic: long quiet periods punctuated by restructuring events that re-identify a large share of the catalogue at once. The intensional clock, characterized by $\tau$, is continuous and has barely varied since 2020. Section 6 estimates both.

The consequence is that the two clocks give opposite answers to the practitioner's question, *how long is my pinned label set good for?* — infinite on identifiers for any post-2020 Enterprise cohort, and roughly eighteen months to first 10% substantive staleness. A risk model reporting only one is wrong in a predictable direction, and the direction depends on which one it reports.

### 4.6 Ontology drift as a threat to validity

We can now place the phenomenon in the experimental-methodology tradition. Temporal bias is the failure to respect the arrow of time in partitioning; its control is the time-aware split [67]. Spatial bias is the failure to respect deployment base rates; its control is a realistic test-set class ratio [67]. Sampling bias, label inaccuracy, data snooping and inappropriate baselines each have a recognized name and control [35]. Ontology drift is the failure to hold the label space fixed across the comparisons a paper makes, and it damages validity in four distinguishable ways.

*Construct validity.* The label is supposed to denote a behaviour. After O6 the same label denotes a different behaviour, so the construct measured is not the construct the labels name. A system trained on a technique as defined in 2020 and evaluated against gold labels assigned under the 2026 definition is measuring agreement between two constructs.

*Internal validity.* After O4 or O5 a label set's cardinality and granularity change under normalization. A group profile containing four distinct documented behaviours can, after mechanical migration, contain three — its measured breadth falls with no change in the underlying intelligence. Any analysis treating profile size as a feature inherits the artefact.

*External validity.* After O1 the denominator of any coverage or recall claim grows. An unchanged capability reports a falling number; if the denominator shrinks it reports a rising one. Neither movement is about the world.

*Conclusion validity.* This is the one that matters for a journal. If the effect were confined to scores the appropriate response would be an error bar. Section 7.3 shows it reaches the named actor, the top-ranked mitigation and the pass/fail verdict on a coverage target.

The control for ontology drift is not a better algorithm. It is declaration plus projection: state the release, the domain and the bundle hash, and project both sides of any cross-time comparison onto one reference release with a published residual. Section 10.2 gives the contract; Sections 8 and 11 state how far it goes and where it stops.

### 4.7 What a valid measurement of drift requires

Because drift is a threat to validity, a study of drift is unusually exposed to its own subject. We commit in advance to four requirements, each audited in Section 11. **R1. Fixed labelling process across conditions** — the identifying contrast must hold everything constant except the vocabulary, or it is confounded with intelligence content. **R2. A stated noise model** — holding noise at zero establishes separability and says nothing about additivity; a study claiming additivity must vary the noise rate. **R3. A declared closure boundary** — if observation, profile, ground truth and migration map derive from one curator, the absolute levels are internal-consistency scores and must never be reported as task performance. **R4. A named falsifier.**

### 5.2 Measurements

Seven families support the argument. **E1** computes per-release churn: live counts, additions, revocations, deprecations, renames, description and detection rewrites, tactic reassignments, identifier Jaccard and edge deltas. **E2** computes identifier survival to v19.0 with recoverability through the transitive closure of the revocation graph. **E3** restricts to identifier-stable techniques and measures intensional drift by token Jaccard over descriptions. **E4** decomposes each newly added group-technique edge into genuine new intelligence, an edge to a new technique, a sub-technique refinement, or a revocation re-mapping. **E5** is the controlled attribution experiment. **E6** is the frozen-capability coverage experiment with a prevalence-weighted variant. **E7** dates four deployed CTI corpora against every release. **E10** re-runs attribution and mitigation ranking at the level of conclusions rather than scores. Auxiliary analyses cover version-metadata reliability, the v19 revocation wave, the tactic layer, noise injection, stratification and corpus integrity.

### 5.3 The attribution design and its contrast logic

Fix a modern release $W$ = v19.0 and a legacy release $V$. Take the threat groups documented at $W$ with their technique profiles. Per trial, sample a group and draw $k = 10$ observed techniques from its modern profile, then back-project each into $\mathcal{L}_V$: pre-revocation identifier where one exists, surviving ancestor where the sub-technique did not yet exist, drop where no $V$-era ancestor exists. This yields a $V$-vocabulary observation of a $W$-era intrusion.

Four conditions score it. **Back-projected** (labelled *contemporaneous* in our released code and tables) scores the $V$-vocabulary observation against $V$-vocabulary profiles — self-consistent, no mismatch. **Naive** scores it directly against $W$-vocabulary profiles — the analyst who ignores drift. **ATT&CK-Norm** applies the Section 8 protocol first. **Oracle** scores the original $W$-vocabulary observation against $W$ profiles.

The *drift penalty* is back-projected minus naive accuracy; *recovery* is the fraction of that penalty normalization returns. Every condition consumes the same intelligence content and the same draws, satisfying R1: the contrast cannot be explained by a difference in what was observed. Because all four draw labels from the same curator's data, inter-labeller noise is identically zero in all of them and cancels in the contrast; Section 7.2 varies it. Robustness is assessed across three scoring functions and two profile definitions (Table 7).

### 5.4 Evidence discipline

This paper's subject is the reliability of what artefacts declare about themselves, so we state our own tiers. **Tier A** covers our measurements over the bundles and the repository artefacts we read in full; these are stated plainly and reproduced by the released code. **Tier B** covers secondary literature reached in this environment only through search summaries, because the egress path blocks publisher sites and preprint servers; every Tier B claim is attributed as reported, and no verbatim quotation is asserted from a source we did not read in full. **Tier C** covers audited absences, stated with the bound of the search that found them and never upgraded to proofs. Section 11.6 reports what this cost.
